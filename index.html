Tethered melody

A tethered drone can spin faster than hover while the line takes up the extra thrust, so it can play any note from its hover note up to full throttle. It can't go below its hover note or fall silent while airborne: between notes it rests on the hover note, a bit like a bagpipe drone. Repeated notes are articulated with a short thrust dip.

Chord progression

Collective-pitch drones change note by changing head speed while the flight controller adjusts blade pitch to keep them hovering. The progression uses the root note and tuning from the Chord settings; in just intonation each chord is tuned pure against its own root.

How the model works
  • Hover RPM comes from thrust balance: each rotor carries a quarter of the weight, T = CT·ρ·n²·D⁴. CT is estimated from pitch-to-diameter ratio and blade count, so it is typical of hobby props rather than any specific brand.
  • The fundamental tone is the blade passing frequency, blades × rev/s. Each rotor also carries a weak shaft-rate tone from imbalance and a motor tone at 7× shaft rate (14-pole motor).
  • The four rotors never spin at exactly the same speed: yaw trim and centre-of-gravity offset split them by about ±0.5%, and the flight controller adds random speed corrections that grow with wind.
  • Noise level at 1 m scales with tip speed and weight, fitted to published measurements of small multirotors. Treat absolute levels as ±5 dB estimates; relative changes are more reliable.
  • Outdoors: spherical spreading, ISO 9613-1 style air absorption, and ground reflection from an image source (interference ripples in the spectrum). Indoors: direct field plus a diffuse reverberant field from Sabine's formula, and a matching reverb in the audio.
  • Collective-pitch drones: a governor holds the commanded head speed and the flight controller sets blade pitch so thrust equals weight, with CT rising roughly linearly with pitch angle. Usable hover collective is taken as 3.5°–9° (below that there is little margin to descend or control, above it there is no margin to climb; maximum collective is 14°), and head speed must stay within 25–85% of the motor's maximum. Head speed follows the command with a first-order lag plus a slew limit, which is why note changes glide.
  • Tethered drones: thrust may exceed weight because the tether carries the difference, so RPM can rise from the hover value up to the motor's loaded maximum. Tension = total thrust − weight. Motor speed changes follow a lag of about 30 ms for 5-inch props, growing with prop size, and slowing down is 1.6× slower than speeding up.
  • Hover power uses momentum theory for induced power plus blade profile drag, divided by 70% motor and ESC efficiency.
  • Throttle, thrust-to-weight and tip Mach flags show whether a configuration could actually fly.
